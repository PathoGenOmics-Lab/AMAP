Everything lands under --outdir, results/ by default, in one subdirectory per pipeline stage. {sampleId} below is the sample's name as lineage typing leaves it, L4_G26689 for sample G26689, or the sample ID alone under --skip_typing.

The key files

#

*-AMAP.gff3 and *-AMAP.gbk

#

The corrected annotation, which is the point of the pipeline. The GFF3 is spec-compliant: gene features parent their CDS features, attributes are URL-escaped, ##sequence-region pragmas are emitted per contig, and eggNOG annotation rides along as Ontology_term, eC_number, COG_category, KEGG_ko, KEGG_Pathway and PFAMs. The sequences are appended after ##FASTA.

Two kinds of gene carry marks, written the way NCBI writes them:

  • Pseudogenes have pseudo=true in the GFF3 and /pseudo in the GenBank, with the reason in Note and /note: internal stop codons, a frameshift or lost stop that makes the frame run on past the end of the reference protein, no stop at all, or a stop the reference itself lacks. A /pseudo CDS has no /translation.
  • Genes a contig end cut short, in a draft assembly, have partial=true, with start_range=.,START or end_range=END,. on the open end, and < or > on that end of the GenBank location. They are not pseudogenes. Each piece of a cut gene is there under the gene's name, the first in the assembly with the gene's identifier and the others with _2, _3.

The same three facts, pseudogene, partial (5', 3' or both) and note, are the last columns of the master table and of *-AMAP.tsv.

A gene across the origin of a circular sequence is written the way Bakta writes it. Bakta takes a sequence for a circular replicon when its FASTA description says complete, as NCBI's "complete genome" and "complete sequence" do, or circular=true, as Unicycler's does. In *-AMAP.tsv and the master table such a gene has its start after its end (stcE on plasmid pO157 of E. coli O157:H7 Sakai is 92563 to 2502); in the GenBank it is join(92563..92721,1..2502) in a record marked circular; and in the GFF3 it ends past the sequence's last base, by the sequence's length (92563 to 95223 on the 92721 bp plasmid), on a region feature marked Is_circular=true, as the GFF3 specification and Bakta's own GFF3 have it.

*-AMAP.faa versus *-AMAP.clean.faa

#

*-AMAP.faa mirrors the annotation exactly, pseudogenes included, so gene counts match the GFF3.

*-AMAP.clean.faa drops the genes AMAP calls pseudogenes, those with pseudo=true in the GFF3, and any sequence with an internal stop codon, and strips the terminal stop. This is the file for orthology, phylogenomics, pangenome tools and dN/dS, all of which expect valid translations. It has fewer sequences than the GFF3 has genes, by design.

*-AMAPmastertable.tsv

#

One row per gene, joining the corrected coordinates, the Bakta annotation, the eggNOG assignment (COG category, description, preferred name, GO, EC, KEGG, PFAMs, CAZy) and, for MTBC, the Mycobrowser functional category. This is the table to load when you want to ask a question about the annotation.

*-AMAP.qc.tsv

#

One row per sample: genome size, contig count, N50, GC%, total CDS, how many of them AMAP calls pseudogenes (those with pseudo=true in the GFF3), hypothetical protein percentage, mean and median gene length, coding density, and the eggNOG annotation rate. Useful for spotting the sample that went wrong.

CDS_CASES/

#

The audit trail for the correction. Each case table lists the genes that fell into it, so you can see exactly what was changed and why. The correction algorithm explains the cases; *-summary.tsv aggregates the counts across samples and feeds the report. Its last three columns say which sample the counts are for: Sequence is the name its files carry (L4_G26689), Lineage what typing called it (Untyped under --skip_typing) and Sample its ID in the sample sheet (G26689).

What a healthy summary looks like

#

Two real runs, those of the tutorial, to calibrate expectations. On the left, M. tuberculosis CDC1551 against the bundled H37Rv reference. On the right, E. coli O157:H7 Sakai against K-12 MG1655, using a custom reference.

CaseCDC1551 vs H37RvSakai vs K-12
case2, exact match28133324
case3, length differs2431
case4, resolved conflict1100507
case1_kept, genuinely novel111387
rv_missing, rescued97133
total_cds before correction40775367

The one number that differs by two orders of magnitude is case 1, and that is biology: CDC1551 is the same species as the reference so almost nothing is genuinely new, while O157:H7 carries prophages and the LEE island that K-12 does not have.

What should worry you is the opposite: case 2 at or near zero. Every gene disagreeing with the reference by a few bases means something systematic is wrong with the reference, not with 4000 individual genes. The usual cause is a reference proteome whose coordinates do not describe the sequence its proteins were translated from.

PANGENE/pangenome.Rtab

#

The gene presence/absence matrix of the whole run, the one both reports draw the pangenome from: one row per gene, one column per sample, 1 where pangene found the gene in that sample's genome. Every genome is searched with the same proteins, the reference proteome and the genes no reference gene matches, so a gene with a 1 in every column is one all the samples share. A gene is named after its reference gene (Rv1506c) or, when no reference gene matches it, after one sample's copy of it (L4_CDC1551_01396, the ID in that sample's master table).

final_report.html

#

Self-contained, so it travels by email. Covers per-sample quality, missing reference genes by lineage, PE/PPE family distribution, KEGG pathways, duplication communities and network topology, the pangenome (core, softcore, shell, cloud, frequency spectrum, presence/absence heatmaps), COG and Mycobrowser distributions, case statistics and the associated tests, plus a searchable master-table explorer.

amap_report.html

#

One self-contained file, organised by the questions a run raises: how each genome annotated, where genes are missing or broken on the reference, one gene in every genome, what the correction changed, the pseudogenes by lineage, what lies outside the reference, and the functional profile. Written with or without the functional annotation. The interactive report goes through it.

Effect of the skip options

#
  • --skip_typing: No PATHOTYPR/ directory. Samples keep their original IDs instead of being renamed by lineage.
  • --skip_functional_annotation: The run stops after correction. No DUPLICATIONS/, PANGENE/ or final_report.html, and AMAP/ holds only the corrected table and sequences, *.AMAP.tsv, *.AMAP.faa and *.AMAP.fna, without the GFF3, the GenBank or the master table. You still get BAKTA/ and CDS_CASES/, including *_corrected.tsv with the corrected coordinates, and amap_report.html, without its eggNOG sections.
  • --skip_mycobrowser: *-AMAP.MycoBrowser.tsv is written empty and the Mycobrowser sections drop out of the report. Everything else is unaffected.
