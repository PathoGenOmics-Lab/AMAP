Docs / How it works

The workflow

The pipeline as a metro map: stations are the processes of main.nf, lines are what travels between them. Pick a station for what it does and where its files go, or switch an option to see what a run leaves out.

1. eggNOG database setup#

Checks whether the eggNOG database exists at --eggnog_db and builds it if not. A bacteria.dmnd under 100 MB is treated as a truncated download and rebuilt.

The database is large, so point --eggnog_db at persistent shared storage on a cluster. Skipped entirely under --skip_functional_annotation.

2. Lineage typing#

pathotypr assigns each genome to an MTBC lineage using phylogenetic marker SNPs, and the genomes are renamed after their sample ID with the lineage in front (L4_G26689 for sample G26689). That prefix carries through every downstream file and is what lets the report group results by lineage.

pathotypr types each contig of a draft on its own, and the lineage of the draft is the one the markers of all its contigs add up to. A genome of one sequence has that sequence renamed after the sample and keeps the description that follows the name in its header, so Bakta still reads a closed genome as complete and circular when the description says so (NCBI's "complete genome" and "complete sequence", Unicycler's circular=true); the contigs of a draft keep their names. A genome with no lineage markers at all is named NA_G26689.

MTBC-specific. Turn it off with --skip_typing for anything else, and sample IDs stay as you wrote them.

3. Annotation with Bakta#

Bakta produces the baseline annotation: CDS, tRNA, rRNA and other features. This is the annotation AMAP will correct, and it is published unmodified under BAKTA/ so you can compare.

--genus and --species are passed through, so setting them correctly improves the starting point.

4. Protein alignment with Miniprot#

Miniprot aligns the reference proteome onto each genome, spliced and frameshift-aware. Two alignments are produced: the reference proteome, and the false-positive set that will filter novel-gene candidates.

Output is PAF. Coordinates are converted from 0-based to 1-based on read, and for a protein hitting several positions, the best hit per position is kept so multi-copy families such as PE/PPE keep all their placements.

In a draft assembly the genes a contig end cuts get a second pass. Miniprot places the larger piece of such a gene only, so what that placement lacks is aligned again as a protein of its own, and its placement at the end of another contig is the rest of the gene (pieces of cut genes).

5. Case classification#

Every Bakta CDS is compared against the alignments and sorted into cases 1 to 4, plus the reference genes Bakta missed. Comparisons are strand-aware throughout: a gene at (100, 200, +) and one at (100, 200, -) are different genes and are never merged.

Tables land in CDS_CASES/, one per case, plus interactive overlap plots. The correction algorithm covers the classification.

6. Correction#

The cases are resolved into one corrected coordinate set: conflicts adjudicated, novel genes filtered, missing reference genes rescued, duplicates removed.

Then check_cds.py translates every surviving CDS. A gene that does not end in a stop codon has its coordinates extended to the next in-frame stop, up to 300 bp. Genes with internal stops are flagged as pseudogenes rather than discarded. Anything that cannot be translated at all is dropped, so the annotation table and the FASTA files always agree.

Outputs are the corrected gene table and its protein and nucleotide files under AMAP/ (*.AMAP.tsv, .faa, .fna). The GFF3 and GenBank files follow once eggNOG has run, in step 7.

7. Functional annotation#

eggNOG-mapper assigns COG categories, KEGG orthologs and pathways, GO terms, EC numbers, PFAM domains and CAZy families.

This runs once over all samples together, not once per sample. With closely related genomes most proteins are identical across samples, so they are deduplicated first, annotated once, and the results propagated back. For a few hundred MTBC genomes that cuts the Diamond search by an order of magnitude and avoids hundreds of jobs contending for the same database on a shared filesystem.

The results are merged into *-AMAPmastertable.tsv and into the GFF3 attributes.

8. Duplication analysis#

Each proteome is searched against itself with Diamond to find within-genome duplications, which are grouped into families by connected components. Then the proteins of every sample's families are pooled and searched all against all to build homologous communities across genomes, with network metrics computed per community.

Duplication metrics explains the numbers.

9. Pangenome#

pangene builds a pangenome graph from the corrected annotations and produces a gene presence/absence matrix, which the report turns into core, softcore, shell and cloud classifications, a frequency spectrum and presence/absence heatmaps.

Each sample's corrected proteins are aligned with miniprot onto every other sample's genome: the ones PATHOTYPR renamed or, with --skip_typing, the genomes of the sample sheet, each under its sample ID.

10. Report#

A single self-contained HTML file built from every output above. Sample quality, missing reference genes by lineage, PE/PPE families, KEGG pathways, duplication communities and topology, the pangenome, COG and Mycobrowser distributions, case statistics with the associated tests, and a searchable table of every gene.

Alongside it, pipeline_info/ collects the Nextflow execution report, timeline, DAG and trace, plus software_versions.yml and params.yml for your methods section.
